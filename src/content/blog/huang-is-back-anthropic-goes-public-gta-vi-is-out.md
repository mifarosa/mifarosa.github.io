This week, a CEO left and returned within 11 days. The largest IPO in history took place. A game fans had waited 13 years for finally launched, and PlayStation Network had problems around its release.

#### 1. Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO on November 12 over a scandal involving his personal life, which also raised national-security questions. At the company's earnings call on November 17, board chair Harvey Jones said the board did not intend to reinstate him. On November 23, the board voted unanimously to bring Huang back as president, CEO and board member. According to the company's SEC filing, the board said it was satisfied with Huang's candor and his ability to lead. Nvidia also said it will hire a Chief Compliance Officer. The stock closed about 31% higher that day and fell 5.86% the next day. According to reports, congressional scrutiny of the company continues.

#### 2. Anthropic goes public

Anthropic, the company behind Claude, began trading on Nasdaq on November 19 under the ticker "ANT." Shares were priced at $83 and opened at $111.50. The company raised about $81 billion, surpassing SpaceX's June listing as the largest IPO in history. Its prospectus includes dozens of pages of risk factors, including the possibility that AI could act outside human control.

#### 3. GTA VI launches as PSN struggles

Grand Theft Auto VI launched on November 19 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. It is the series' first new main entry in 13 years. On November 18, hours before release, PlayStation Network users reported problems with sign-in and downloads. A second, shorter disruption followed during the launch. There is still no official release date for the PC version.

#### 4. Patch list

- **Chrome 158** (November 17): 204 security fixes, six rated critical. Versions are 158.0.8127.36/.37 on desktop and 158.0.8127.42 on Android. Google credits two of the critical bugs to a researcher who used AI to find them.
- **Chrome 157.0.8103.50/.51** (November 9, for context): fixed CVE-2026-125350, a zero-day already being exploited in attacks. It is the eighth Chrome zero-day of the year.
- **Microsoft Patch Tuesday** (November 10, for context): more than 1,000 vulnerabilities fixed, including CVE-2026-124194, an Outlook flaw being actively exploited. Fixing it requires installing the separate Office update.
- **Windows 11 hotpatch KB5132043** (November 19): Microsoft confirmed that apps can fail to install or launch on Windows 11 24H2 and later after this update, and shared a workaround.
- **Cisco Expressway** (November 23): CISA said CVE-2026-76466 is being actively exploited.
- **Firefox 161:** scheduled for release on November 24. Firefox 160, released November 10, fixed 99 security vulnerabilities.
- **Apple iOS/iPadOS 27.2.1 and macOS 27.2.1** (November 9, for context): nine security fixes.

To update Chrome, go to Menu → Help → About Google Chrome → Relaunch.

#### 5. This week's breaches

On November 24, Turnitin, the plagiarism-checking tool used by universities, disclosed unauthorized activity on some of its customer-facing systems. The European Commission confirmed that personal data was stolen from its EU Academy online learning platform. Security company Radware said some customers' cloud access keys were exposed through an internal API that had been left open online.

#### 6. Which side are you on? Tidal is removing AI-generated music

In an announcement on November 24, Tidal said that starting December 9 it will begin removing fully AI-generated tracks from its catalog and will reject new uploads of such tracks. According to the company, 43% of tracks delivered in the past 90 days were fully AI-generated. About 27 million tracks in its catalog carry that label, which is 6.3% of the total. Tidal executive Robert Andersen said 96% of these tracks got zero plays in the last three months. Music made only partly with AI is not affected.

**Those in favor of the ban** say it protects real artists' royalties, keeps listeners from being misled by impersonations, and preserves the quality of the catalog.
**Skeptics** point out that other platforms so far only label AI music. In June, Tidal itself said listeners should be free to choose what they hear. It is also unclear how accurate detection tools are.

**Your take:** Should fully AI-generated music be banned, or is a label enough?
